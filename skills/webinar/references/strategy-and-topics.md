# Strategy and Topics

## Match format to outcome

| Outcome | Best format | Why |
|---------|-------------|-----|
| Demo requests / pipeline | Solo or two-person teaching session with a live example | Tight story, single offer |
| Authority and reach | Interview or expert panel | Guests bring their audiences |
| Adoption and retention | Workshop or hands-on training | Attendees do the thing live |
| Content production | Any format, recorded for reuse | The recording is the product |
| Co-hosted reach | Partner session | See **co-marketing** for partner mechanics |

## Finding topics

1. List the top 10 objections and questions from sales calls and support tickets.
2. Keep those that a prospect has before they know your product exists (problem-aware).
3. Check demand: do people search for it, ask about it in communities, or did a past post on it perform?
4. Reject topics that need your product to make sense. The webinar should be useful if the viewer never buys.

## Title patterns

- How [role] can [outcome] without [pain] (for example, "How startup CFOs cut close time without hiring")
- [Number] [things] that [audience] get wrong about [topic]
- Live teardown: [specific artifact] for [audience]

Test three candidates as email subject lines or social posts and pick the one that earns the most clicks.

## Offer design

- The offer should be the natural next step of the content: a demo, trial, template, audit, or consultation.
- Write the bridge sentence now: "Everything I showed took X hours by hand; here is how the product does it."
- Make the offer visible on the thank-you page, in reminders, and at the close.

## Sizing the goal

Work backwards with your own rates: pipeline target ÷ conversion from attendee to opportunity ÷ show-up rate = registrants needed. Then check that your reach (list, partners, paid) can plausibly supply them. If not, shrink the goal or extend the promo window.
