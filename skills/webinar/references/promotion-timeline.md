# Promotion Timeline

## Registration page

- Headline: the outcome for the audience, not the event name.
- 3 to 4 bullets of what attendees will walk away with, plus speaker names and credentials.
- Date, time with time zone, and duration. Short form (name, work email, one qualifying question at most).
- Offer a replay to registrants who cannot attend live.
- Thank-you page: add-to-calendar, share link, and a related resource.

## Channels by week

| Channel | Use |
|---------|-----|
| Email list | Announcement, second angle, last chance, three reminders |
| In-product and customer comms | Existing users who match the topic |
| Social (founder and speaker accounts) | Teasers, speaker quotes, countdown |
| Partners and guests | Ready-to-send copy and a tracked link each |
| Sales outreach | A genuine invite to open opportunities, not a bulk blast |
| Paid or sponsored newsletters | Only when the list cannot reach the goal |

## Email angles (promo)

1. Announcement: the problem and the outcome.
2. A different angle: a story, a number, or a customer example.
3. Speaker or guest spotlight.
4. Last chance with a content teaser.
5. Reminders: day before, one hour before, five minutes before (join link only).

## Show-up system

- Send a calendar invite at registration.
- Keep the gap between registration and event short for late registrants (offer on-demand alongside).
- Ask a pre-event question and use it in the session.
- Pick the slot from your audience's habits; test mid-week mornings and early afternoons in their time zone.

## Pacing check

Compare registrations to plan at the end of weeks -3 and -2. If behind, change the angle, add a partner push, or widen the audience before adding spend. Tag every link with UTMs so you can see which channels register and which attend.
